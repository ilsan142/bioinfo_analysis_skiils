# Revise slides for a live research presentation

Read this when revising supplied slides, reducing stiff or translated wording, or proposing slide-level layout and chart changes. Work from the actual supplied content and source evidence. Do not infer AI authorship from visual style. A request for clearer slides authorizes presentation edits, not new analysis or stronger scientific claims.

## Make the main message immediately visible

- Put one short sentence at the top that tells the audience what the evidence supports. Prefer a specific subject and observation over vague headings such as "Contribution of the analysis" or a question used only to create suspense.
- Use natural phrasing appropriate to a spoken research presentation. Follow the user's language; do not impose English sentence structure on Korean text. Keep precise scientific terms when a simpler replacement would change the meaning.
- When a result is unresolved, say so directly or retain a genuine research question. Do not turn an uncertain estimate into a positive finding to make the headline more forceful.
- Treat recognition within roughly three seconds as an editing goal. Check that the headline, main visual, and one emphasis point agree; do not claim that audience comprehension or speaking time was tested unless it was.

## Let the comparison determine the layout

Describe where the audience should look first and what they should compare next. Give a concrete arrangement for the supplied content, not a generic instruction to "make it cleaner."

- For one main result, give the evidence panel most of the space and place a short annotation beside the relevant observation.
- For matched conditions, aligned panels or a paired display can make the comparison clear. Retain comparable scales and visible independent observations.
- For alternatives, use a compact comparison table when the audience needs to weigh the same criteria for each option.
- For a sequence or design, use an ordered diagram with the measured and proposed steps distinguished.

These are options, not a required set of slide types. Do not replace every two-column slide with a new repeated template, decorative cards, or extra boxes. Keep an effective existing layout when it serves the evidence. Split an overloaded slide only when it improves the explanation, preserving the original-to-revised slide mapping.

## Shorten labels and body text without changing the science

Keep visible text to the words needed to orient the audience and support the headline. Put procedural narration and explanatory transitions in speaker notes when notes are requested or supplied; do not create a separate long script for a small wording edit.

Check ambiguous labels against the analysis record before renaming them. An odd-to-even label may refer to an index split, a time transition, or something else; do not guess. If its meaning cannot be recovered, retain the original with a brief clarification needed and continue independent edits. Distinguish a decay rate from a half-life, odds from probability, and an odds ratio from a risk ratio. A more familiar word is not a valid substitute if it changes the quantity.

For chart edits, specify the exact replacement labels and the relevant change to scale, legend, annotation, or emphasis. Preserve measurement units, log transformations, reference groups, comparison direction, independent sample counts, and interval definitions. Keep condition colors consistent across slides; use emphasis to guide attention without hiding discordant samples or uncertainty. Follow the parent skill's biological display preferences, including no newly introduced hexbin unless requested.

Remove redundant prose from the bottom of the slide. Put the conditions needed to interpret the figure in a readable compact legend. Move optional methods and full source locators to notes or backup material while keeping a retrievable source link; retain required attribution on the slide. A limitation that changes the headline belongs beside the claim or in the main evidence panel, not only in notes. For example, complete condition/batch confounding cannot be removed as an unattractive footnote.

## Return concrete changes for each requested slide

Use the following four fields, translated into the user's language, unless a different output format was requested:

- **[Slide N] Original title -> revised headline:** one bounded takeaway or an explicitly unresolved question. If a title or slide is missing, say so rather than inventing the original.
- **[Layout proposal]:** placement, relative emphasis, and the comparison or reading order the arrangement supports.
- **[Slide body text and bullets]:** ready-to-use concise wording, not instructions to the presenter. Include short essential conditions where needed.
- **[Chart/visualization changes]:** exact label replacements, color/annotation changes, and any scientific information that must remain visible. Distinguish suggested edits from edits actually made.

Place short source or verification notes within the relevant field rather than requiring another inventory. If no chart is present, say that chart changes are not applicable. For text-only or unrendered inputs, label layout changes as proposals. When a deck is actually edited, open the rendered slides to check readability, overlap, clipping, legends, and consistency with the source; a written revision plan alone does not establish that these checks passed.

## Small wording example

This is synthetic teaching material, not an executed analysis. Suppose a supplied record reports six paired donors, a treated-minus-control mean difference of +0.20 log2 units, and a 95% donor-bootstrap interval of -0.05 to +0.44. The target was selected during exploration.

A vague title such as "Contribution of treatment to target activation" can become "The direction of the treatment-associated difference remains uncertain." A concise on-slide legend can retain "6 paired donors; treated - control; log2 units; 95% donor-bootstrap interval; exploratory target." If donor-level points are available, keep their mixed directions visible. Do not shorten the message to "Treatment activates the target," hide the interval, or claim the plotted points were inspected when only a table was supplied.
